# First 500 paying users

Treat the playbook's 500-user figure as an aspiration, not a forecast. The method is to learn from real public audience questions, publish useful content on the user's own account, and measure qualified signups and payments.

## Workflow

1. Read `docs/references/industry/First-500-Paying-Users-Playbook.txt`; keep the supplied PDF as the source record.
2. Define the product, target customer, geography, and problem using facts the user provides. Do not invent audience demand, customers, revenue, or case studies.
3. Help the user identify up to 20 relevant public creators, using the source's 5K–50K follower range only as a starting heuristic. Do not invent creators or scrape private/login-gated data.
4. Analyze only comments or exports the user supplies or is authorized to use. Group repeated complaints, count evidence, quote accurately, and label the sample size and collection date.
5. Draft one 50–80 second useful video per validated complaint theme. Lead with the customer's real wording, provide useful advice even without the product, then explain one verifiable product action and a clear next step.
6. Check that the landing page addresses the same problem, the price and claims are supported, and the call to action is understandable.
7. Track views, profile visits, signups, and paid conversions per video. Compare conversion per 1,000 views and state small-sample uncertainty; do not optimize on views alone.
8. Recommend what to repeat, change, or stop from the measured data. A creator collaboration, tag, comment, direct message, or publication requires explicit authorization and must follow platform rules.

## Boundaries

- Do not post promotional comments under other creators' content or send unsolicited messages. Use audience language for research and publish on the user's own channels unless a creator has agreed to collaborate.
- Do not fabricate testimonials, results, creator activity, or conversion figures.
- The playbook's arithmetic (20 creators times 25 customers) is a target illustration, not a guarantee.
