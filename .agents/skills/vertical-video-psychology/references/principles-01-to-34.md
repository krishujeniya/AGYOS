# Vertical Video Principles: 1 to 34

---

## Topic 1: The Opening and the Decision to Stay (Principles 1–11)

1. **Viewing Motives**: People enter feeds for entertainment, escape, novelty, or information.  
   *Edit Directive*: Align the opening frame directly with the core motive (solve a problem, deliver a punchline, or invite active participation). Avoid generic introductions. [V03, V19]
2. **Incidental Discovery**: Viewers encounter clips cold without knowing the creator or context.  
   *Edit Directive*: Provide concrete anchor nouns immediately. Replace abstract hype ("This changes everything") with specific utility ("This setting changes how your microphone records speech"). [V06]
3. **Goal-Directed Attention**: Attention is shaped by what the viewer is actively seeking.  
   *Edit Directive*: For technical or instructional videos, show the recognized symptom on-screen before demonstrating the solution steps. [R01]
4. **First Impressions**: Early visual features disproportionately govern initial engagement.  
   *Edit Directive*: Test opening on the critical failure condition versus the finished payoff; keep subsequent shots identical. [V02]
5. **Orienting to a New Onset**: Abrupt visual onsets capture selective attention.  
   *Edit Directive*: Introduce essential visual objects into the frame at the precise moment their presence or absence matters; do not leave them static in the background. [R01, R02]
6. **Habituation**: Repetitive, non-informative sensory stimuli experience rapid response decay.  
   *Edit Directive*: Eliminate formulaic whip-zooms, chimes, or sound effects on routine lines; reserve sensory emphasis for genuine state changes. [R03]
7. **Information-Gap Curiosity**: Curiosity activates when viewers perceive a specific gap in existing knowledge.  
   *Edit Directive*: Formulate concrete comparisons: "Both exports used the same source file—why is the second render half the size?" Answer it directly within the clip. [R20, R21]
8. **Specificity of a Tease**: Extreme vagueness and total premature disclosure both suppress interest.  
   *Edit Directive*: Provide calibrated detail: "Why the last subtitle vanished on export" rather than vague claims or giving away the technical setting in frame one. [R22, R83]
9. **Manageable Novelty**: Novel concepts maintain engagement when viewers believe they can comprehend them.  
   *Edit Directive*: Position an unfamiliar concept beside a familiar anchor (e.g. an uncompressed waveform beside its audible peak). [R23]
10. **Surprise and Expectation Violation**: Discrepancies direct focus when an explicit baseline expectation exists.  
    *Edit Directive*: Establish two identical conditions first, then reveal the single consequential anomaly; allow sufficient viewer dwell time to perceive the divergence. [R86]
11. **Self-Reference**: Information is retained more effectively when linked to viewer identity or experience.  
    *Edit Directive*: Frame options around viewer realities: "Which of these two audio problems matches your home setup?" before presenting the comparative trade-offs. [R28]

## Topic 2: Composition Inside a Vertical Frame (Principles 12–23)

12: **Portrait Orientation and Physical Effort**: Vertical orientation minimizes mechanical friction on mobile screens.  
    *Edit Directive*: Reframe widescreen assets natively for 9:16 portrait. When displaying dense software interfaces, cut to a legible close-up of the active control rather than letterboxing. [V01]
13. **Interface Overlay Competition**: Platform UI (like buttons, usernames, comment bars, captions) obscures screen regions.  
    *Edit Directive*: Maintain a safe zone: place critical demonstration objects, pricing numbers, and action points clear of bottom and right platform overlays. [V09, O06]
14. **Figure-Ground Segregation**: Visual elements require defined luminance or color contrast against their backgrounds.  
    *Edit Directive*: Place high-contrast backing behind small or dark demonstration objects. Verify legibility at standard phone screen scale. [R06]
15. **Visual Crowding**: Flanking peripheral clutter impairs focal object recognition.  
    *Edit Directive*: Crop terminal outputs, code snippets, or interfaces down to the essential lines; leave negative space around key variables. [R93]
16. **Proximity**: Spatially contiguous elements are grouped perceptually.  
    *Edit Directive*: Position callout labels and pricing directly adjacent to their referenced visual objects to avoid gaze searching. [R06]
17. **Similarity and Visual Grouping**: Elements sharing geometric form or styling are categorized as related.  
    *Edit Directive*: Maintain identical bounding box styles for input variables, and distinct uniform styles for output states. [R06]
18. **Common Fate**: Elements moving with identical velocity and vector are bound together perceptually.  
    *Edit Directive*: Motion-track callout labels to the specific physical part they identify rather than letting them float statically. [R06]
19. **Gaze Cueing and Joint Attention**: Presenter gaze vectors naturally guide audience fixation.  
    *Edit Directive*: Direct the presenter's gaze toward the focal object immediately prior to cutting to its detail close-up. [R05]
20. **Inattentional Blindness**: High task focus causes viewers to miss unattended unexpected visual events.  
    *Edit Directive*: When secondary actions occur during a primary task, isolate the secondary event in a dedicated replay shot. [R04]
21. **Signaling**: Explicit visual callouts reduce cognitive search overhead.  
    *Edit Directive*: Apply a single subtle bounding box or pointer directly onto the changing interface toggle at the exact moment of mutation. [R09]
22. **Contrast and Redundant Coding**: Information encoded solely by color excludes color-deficient viewers.  
    *Edit Directive*: Render captions with high-contrast background backings (minimum 4.5:1 luminance ratio). Encode errors using text or symbols alongside color changes. [O09, O10]
23. **Color in Context**: Color associations depend on culture, task context, and learned semantics.  
    *Edit Directive*: Explicitly label time-series data ("before" vs "after") rather than relying on assumed emotional interpretations of red and green. [R64, O10]

## Topic 3: Voice, Captions, Music, and Timing (Principles 24–34)

24. **Synchronized Captions**: Timed text enables muted comprehension and reinforces spoken vocabulary.  
    *Edit Directive*: Verify line-by-line accuracy for proper nouns, negation markers ("cannot" vs "can"), numbers, and technical terms. Transcribe meaningful off-screen audio cues. [R11, O08]
25. **Language Matching**: Same-language captions strengthen listening and word boundary recognition.  
    *Edit Directive*: Transcribe exact spoken dialect and code-switching verbatim; preserve technical UI terminology in its on-screen system language. [R12, R75]
26. **Visible Speech in Noise**: Visual speech cues (lip and facial kinematics) increase speech intelligibility in ambient noise.  
    *Edit Directive*: Retain the presenter's lower face in frame when pronouncing unfamiliar technical names; verify cut clarity through mobile speaker output. [R13, R11]
27. **Meaningful Gesture**: Manual gestures communicate spatial relationships, orientations, and magnitudes.  
    *Edit Directive*: Frame manual demonstrations so hand rotations, scale indications, and directions remain fully visible within the vertical crop. [R14]
28. **Prosodic Grouping and Emphasis**: Vocal pitch contours, stress, and pauses delineate grammatical and logical boundaries.  
    *Edit Directive*: Structure verbal edits around natural syntactic pauses; avoid truncating breathing or trailing inflection that signals ongoing thought. [R16]
29. **Lyric Interference**: Vocals in background music directly compete with narrated verbal processing.  
    *Edit Directive*: Use instrumental audio tracks during dense explanations or instructional sequences; avoid vocal tracks that conflict with speech comprehension. [R15, R74]
30. **Temporal Expectation**: Regular rhythmic patterns allow the auditory cortex to predict event onsets.  
    *Edit Directive*: Establish a steady temporal pulse prior to introducing off-beat or syncopated instructional actions. [R18]
31. **Music and Emotional Appraisal**: Expressive musical structures shape affective evaluation.  
    *Edit Directive*: Test narrative scenes against silence, subtle ambient tones, and dynamic cues; select the audio layer that matches the presenter's intended tone without exaggeration. [R17]
32. **Audio Quality and Credibility**: Poor signal-to-noise ratio and room reverberation lower perceived authority and truthfulness.  
    *Edit Directive*: Record clean close-mic audio; apply acoustic room treatment and de-reverberation before publishing. [R19]
33. **Playback Speed and Intelligibility**: Excessive speech acceleration degrades comprehension on complex instructional tasks.  
    *Edit Directive*: Remove dead space and false starts between sentences first; do not compress technical explanations past natural conversational tempo. [R34, R12]
34. **Nonvisual Access**: Information conveyed solely through silent gestures or on-screen graphics excludes vision-impaired or listening-only viewers.  
    *Edit Directive*: Narrate numerical metrics and essential state changes aloud ("the latency dropped from 18ms to 12ms") while displaying them. [O08, O10]
