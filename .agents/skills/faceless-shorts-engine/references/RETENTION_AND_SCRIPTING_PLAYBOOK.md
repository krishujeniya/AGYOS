# Script and timing guidance for faceless shorts

Use these as editorial choices, not platform guarantees. The source package supplies examples and
style-specific constraints; it does not establish a universal retention formula.

## Script

1. Start with the actual question, anomaly, or stakes when it suits the subject. Avoid a long
   greeting, but do not promise a shocking result the story cannot support.
2. Write connected spoken sentences. Read them aloud or synthesize a draft and revise awkward
   phrasing. The source skill suggests about 90–110 words for a 35-second example; measure the
   generated narration instead of treating that estimate as an exact duration rule.
3. Build a clear progression: premise, explanation or evidence, complication, and a useful or
   satisfying ending. A loop is optional; never force one at the expense of accuracy or grammar.
4. Keep fact claims and numbers in script.json tied to sources. For health, safety, and history,
   prefer authoritative primary or institutional sources. Do not invent case details, quotes,
   statistics, or medical effects.

## Timing and visual plan

- Use the toolkit's line and word timing outputs. Correct transcription errors before aligning
  shots; generated speech timing and a person's recording are different inputs.
- Let narration structure and the selected style determine shot duration. The source style guides
  include examples around 1.5–3 seconds, but that is not a required cut rate.
- Show one idea at a time. Change the image when it clarifies a new clause, consequence, object,
  or reveal. Leave time for viewers to understand diagrams and read captions.
- Use the caption count, position, colors, and safe area specified by the selected style guide.
  Review at phone-sized scale rather than relying on a single font-size rule.
- Use only sounds that support a visible or narrated event. Keep speech intelligible and check the
  actual mix; do not claim a loudness target without measuring the export.

## Final review

Review a contact sheet from start to end and inspect full-size frames where text, anatomy, maps,
hands, or transitions are dense. Check that:

- the image matches the words at that moment;
- captions spell the intended words and remain legible;
- anatomy, props, geography, and cause-and-effect remain consistent;
- the scene does not assert more than its sources support;
- music and effects do not mask narration; and
- the exported file has the requested duration, dimensions, frame rate, and audible audio track.

Record unverified details as open items. A storyboard, manifest, or successful render command alone
does not prove visual or factual accuracy.
