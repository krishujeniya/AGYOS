# 03D: Negotiation Playbook

> **Core Principle:** Negotiation is not about winning. It is about finding the overlap between what they need and what you can deliver profitably.
>
> *"Be confident even if you are nervous and screaming inside. Sell results, not your service."* — Him Singh

## 1. The Negotiation Mindset

### The BATNA Framework

| Element | Your Position | Their Position |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| **Best Alternative** | Other clients, savings, different projects | Other freelancers, internal team, delay |
| **Walk-Away Price** | Minimum profitable rate | Maximum budget |
| **Target** | Value-based price | Fair market rate |
| **Aspiration** | Premium pricing | Best value |

### The 3 Rules of Freelance Negotiation

| Rule | Application |
|------|-------------|
| **Never negotiate against yourself** | Don't lower price before they object |
| **Trade, don't concede** | Every concession gets a counter-request |
| **Silence is power** | After stating price, stop talking |

### Pre-Negotiation Preparation

Before every negotiation, know:
- [ ] Your walk-away price (minimum acceptable)
- [ ] Your target price (ideal outcome)
- [ ] Your best alternative (what happens if no deal)
- [ ] Their likely budget (from discovery call)
- [ ] Their alternatives (other freelancers, internal team)
- [ ] 3 value points that justify your price

## 2. Objection Response Library

### The 8 Most Common Objections

#### 1. "Your price is too high"

**Don't say:** "I can lower it"  
**Do say:**
```
"What were you expecting to invest? And what scope were you 
imagining at that price?"
```

**Then:**
```
"I can adjust the scope to fit your budget. Which deliverables 
are must-haves vs. nice-to-haves?"
```

**Psychology:** Reframe from "expensive" to "adjustable scope"

---

#### 2. "Your competitor charges less"

**Don't say:** "But I'm better"  
**Do say:**
```
"What does their scope include? Many freelancers quote lower 
prices by excluding deployment, documentation, or monitoring. 

My price includes [specific inclusions], which ensures the system 
works after I leave."
```

**Psychology:** Compare value, not price

---

#### 3. "We don't have the budget right now"

**Don't say:** "When will you?"  
**Do say:**
```
"What's the cost of not solving this in the next 3 months? 
If [pain] continues at [rate], that's [quantified loss].

We could start with the Starter tier ($1,500) to build the 
roadmap, then execute the full project next quarter."
```

**Psychology:** Cost of inaction > cost of action

---

#### 4. "We need to think about it"

**Don't say:** "No problem, take your time"  
**Do say:**
```
"Totally understand. What specific concerns do you have? 
Is it the scope, timeline, or investment?"
```

**Then:**
```
"Would it help if I [specific action: break into phases, 
adjust timeline, provide references]?"
```

**Psychology:** Surface the real objection

---

#### 5. "Can you do a small test first?"

**Don't say:** "Sure, free trial"  
**Do say:**
```
"I offer a paid pilot for $[Amount] that includes [specific 
scope]. This lets us validate the approach with real data 
before the full investment.

If we proceed to the full project, the pilot fee is credited 
toward the total."
```

**Psychology:** Paid pilots filter serious clients

---

#### 6. "We want to own all the IP"

**Don't say:** "Okay, no problem"  
**Do say:**
```
"You own all final deliverables upon full payment. I retain 
rights to general methodologies and pre-existing tools I've 
developed across projects.

This protects both of us — you get full ownership of what 
you paid for, and I can continue improving my frameworks."
```

**Psychology:** Fair IP split, not total surrender

---

#### 7. "We need to run this by [boss/board]"

**Don't say:** "Sure, let me know"  
**Do say:**
```
"Absolutely. What information would be most helpful for them? 
I can prepare a one-page summary of scope, timeline, and ROI 
that you can forward.

Would it help if I joined the next meeting to answer technical 
questions?"
```

**Psychology:** Make them the hero, equip them to sell internally

---

#### 8. "Can you start immediately?"

**Don't say:** "Yes, right away" (unless true)  
**Do say:**
```
"I can begin discovery and planning immediately, with full 
development starting [date]. This actually benefits you — 
I'll use the prep time to research [specific preparation] 
so we hit the ground running.

Rushed projects often require rework. Let's do this right."
```

**Psychology:** Professional boundaries increase perceived value

## 3. Scope Creep Defense

### The Change Request Process

```
Client asks for extra work
        ↓
"Great idea. Let me assess the impact on timeline and budget."
        ↓
[24-hour pause — never agree immediately]
        ↓
"This adds [X] hours and [Y] days. I can do it for $[Z] 
or we can add it to Phase 2. Which works better?"
        ↓
[They choose: pay more, delay, or drop]
        ↓
Document in writing (email or change order)
        ↓
Proceed only after written confirmation
```

### The Scope Creep Phrases

| When They Say... | You Say... |
|------------------|------------|
| "Can you just add...?" | "Let me check the impact on timeline and budget" |
| "It won't take long" | "You're probably right, but let me confirm so we both have clarity" |
| "While you're at it..." | "Happy to — I'll add it to the change request" |
| "The old freelancer did it" | "I scope projects differently to ensure quality. Let me quote this separately" |
| "It's a small change" | "Small changes add up. Let's track them so you have visibility" |

### The Change Order Template

```
Subject: Change Request — [Project Name]

Hi [Name],

Per your request to add [new scope], I've assessed the impact:

**Original SOW:** [Brief description]
**Requested Change:** [New item]
**Impact on Timeline:** [+X days/weeks]
**Impact on Budget:** [+$Amount]
**Updated Total:** [$New Amount]

Options:
1. Approve change order for $[Amount] and [New Timeline]
2. Defer to Phase 2 (separate SOW after current project)
3. Adjust other scope to accommodate (which scope to reduce?)

Please reply with your preference and I'll update the SOW.

Best,
[Your name]
```

## 4. The Art of the Upsell

### Natural Upsell Moments

| Moment | Upsell Opportunity | Script |
|--------|---------------------|--------|
| **During discovery** | "While we're at it..." | "Since I'm already building the pipeline, adding [feature] would only add [X] hours but give you [Y benefit]" |
| **At delivery** | "Now that this is live..." | "This is working great. Have you considered [next capability]?" |
| **At 30-day check** | "Looking at the data..." | "The model is performing well. If we added [enhancement], I estimate [X% improvement]" |
| **Quarterly review** | "For next quarter..." | "Based on your roadmap, [new project] would be a natural next step" |

### The Upsell Framework

```
1. ACKNOWLEDGE current success
   "The churn model is predicting with 89% accuracy..."

2. IDENTIFY adjacent opportunity
   "...and I noticed your LTV data is clean enough to add 
    a customer segmentation model"

3. QUANTIFY the added value
   "This would let you personalize onboarding, which typically 
    improves activation by 15-20%"

4. MAKE IT EASY
   "Since the data pipeline is already built, this would only 
    take 2 weeks and $3,000"

5. ASK FOR THE NEXT STEP
   "Worth exploring in our next check-in?"
```

## 5. Closing Techniques

### The Assumptive Close

```
"Great. I'll send the SOW and invoice for the deposit today. 
Once that's signed and paid, we can kick off Monday. 
Does that timeline work for you?"
```

**When to use:** They've expressed enthusiasm, no major objections

### The Alternative Close

```
"Would you prefer to start with the Starter audit, or go 
straight to the full MVP build?"
```

**When to use:** They're interested but undecided on scope

### The Urgency Close

```
"I have availability starting [date], but my next opening 
after that is [later date]. Would you like to lock in 
the [earlier date]?"
```

**When to use:** Genuine scarcity (don't fake it)

### The Summary Close

```
"Let me make sure I have this right: You need [deliverable], 
delivered by [date], with [specific requirement]. The 
investment is $[Amount], with 50% to start. 

If that all sounds right, I'll send the paperwork now. 
Sound good?"
```

**When to use:** Complex projects, multiple stakeholders

### The "Reply YES" Close [Him Singh Style]

```
"Kindly reply 'YES' to confirm and agree. If you have any 
questions or want to change something, feel free to ask."
```

**When to use:** Email-based agreements, smaller projects

## 6. AI Prompts

### Prompt 1: Objection Response Trainer

```
Act as a negotiation coach.

I just received this objection from a prospect:
"[PASTE OBJECTION]"

Context:
- My price: $[Amount]
- Their budget hint: $[Range]
- Project scope: [DESCRIPTION]
- My experience: [YEARS + RESULTS]

Write 3 response options:
1. Direct and confident (for when you have leverage)
2. Collaborative and flexible (for when you want the relationship)
3. Boundary-setting (for when they're being unreasonable)

Each response must:
- Be under 75 words
- Include one question
- Move the conversation forward
- Protect your value
```

### Prompt 2: Scope Creep Defender

```
Act as a scope management consultant.

Current project:
- SOW scope: [WHAT'S INCLUDED]
- Price: $[Amount]
- Timeline: [WEEKS]

Client just asked for:
"[PASTE REQUEST]"

Draft a response that:
1. Validates their request (not defensive)
2. Explains impact on timeline
3. Explains impact on budget
4. Offers 2-3 alternatives
5. Asks for written confirmation

Tone: Collaborative, professional, firm on boundaries.
```

### Prompt 3: Upsell Opportunity Finder

```
Act as a growth strategist for freelance services.

Current client project:
- What we built: [DESCRIPTION]
- Client's business: [TYPE]
- Their current metrics: [NUMBERS]
- Their stated goals: [OBJECTIVES]

Identify 3 natural upsell opportunities:
1. Immediate (can add to current project)
2. Near-term (30-60 days post-launch)
3. Strategic (next quarter)

For each:
- The opportunity
- Why it fits their goals
- Estimated value to them
- Your price
- How to introduce it
```

## 7. Action Checklist

### Before Every Negotiation

- [ ] Review discovery call notes (quantified pain)
- [ ] Confirm your walk-away price
- [ ] Prepare 3 value justifications
- [ ] Anticipate 3 likely objections
- [ ] Practice responses out loud
- [ ] Set time limit (don't negotiate forever)

### During Negotiation

- [ ] Listen 80%, talk 20%
- [ ] Take notes (shows respect + captures details)
- [ ] Pause after stating price (silence is power)
- [ ] Trade, don't concede (every concession = counter-request)
- [ ] Summarize agreements before ending

### After Negotiation

- [ ] Send summary email within 2 hours
- [ ] Include agreed scope, price, timeline
- [ ] Attach updated SOW if changes were made
- [ ] Set follow-up date
- [ ] Update CRM with outcome

### Weekly Practice

- [ ] Role-play 1 negotiation scenario with peer
- [ ] Review 1 lost deal (what objection killed it?)
- [ ] Refine 1 response based on real feedback
- [ ] Study 1 negotiation resource (book, podcast, video)
